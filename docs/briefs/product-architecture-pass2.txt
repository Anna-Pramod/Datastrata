[Title] DataStrata Product Architecture
[Subtitle] Decision-grade summary of the 11,000-word product design, including the embedded review comments.


[Callout] Central design claim: one product spine, activated in stages, with no architectural rewrite between the first exporter and the thousandth enterprise.

[Heading1] 1. What the product is
DataStrata is a regulatory intelligence platform for sustainability market access. It continuously answers six questions for each customer: what applies, what changed, what must be done and by when, what evidence is required, what happens if nothing is done, and whether the organisation is ready to be checked. The first wedge is an EU market-access desk for exporters; the destination product extends the same objects and logic into enterprise compliance operations and assurance.
[Heading1] 2. The architecture in one model
[TableHeader] Layer
[TableHeader] Purpose
[TableHeader] Non-negotiable rule
[TableText] Shared Regulatory Knowledge Graph
[TableText] DataStrata-owned sources, regulations, provisions, requirements, programs, jurisdictions, changes, applicability conditions and evidence expectations.
[TableText] Built once, maintained once, used by every customer.
[TableText] Private Customer Compliance Graph
[TableText] Each tenant’s entities, products, trade lanes, facts, determinations, obligations, actions, controls, evidence, findings and history.
[TableText] Strictly isolated, attributable and fully exportable.
[TableText] Obligation join
[TableText] Created when a shared requirement is evaluated against private customer facts and bound to the correct entity, product, facility, supplier or trade lane.
[TableText] Everything upstream creates it correctly; everything downstream satisfies and proves it.

Why this matters: the split is the business model expressed as data. Regulatory knowledge does not get copied into every customer account, and customer state never leaks into the shared graph. Maintenance therefore scales with regulatory change, not customer count.

[Heading1] 4. Knowledge, status and AI rules
• Applicability conditions are data, not hard-coded rules: a small analyst-authorable predicate language handles thresholds, dates, set membership, quantities and scope. It can be statically analysed, which enables self-generating onboarding questions and targeted impact propagation.
• Applicability is three-valued: Applies, Does not apply, or Requires judgment. Missing facts are treated separately and requested from the customer. Negative determinations are first-class, dated and explainable.
• Status is derived, never typed in and never generated by AI. Obligations, evidence freshness, controls, deadlines, tasks and findings produce Current, Attention, At Risk or Blocked states with named blockers.
• AI may compose from the warranted record; it may never add to the record. AI drafts regulatory content for expert review and explains or summarises approved objects with citations. Determinations, deadlines, status, impact scoping and evidence acceptance remain deterministic or human-signed.
• Everything is versioned and attributable. Knowledge entries, determinations and posture preserve what was valid in the world and what DataStrata knew at the time.

[Heading1] 6. Review comments absorbed into the design
[TableHeader] Comment
[TableHeader] Resolved design decision
[TableText] Hard-to-source company facts
[TableText] Offer three paths: upload suggested public documents, provide a public document/link, or enter facts manually. OCR/extraction proposes facts; the customer reviews and confirms before anything enters the record.
[TableText] Ranges instead of exact values
[TableText] Use ranges for fast preliminary screening where legal thresholds permit. Ask for exact, sourced values only when the answer is close to a threshold or a definitive determination depends on them. Preliminary outputs are labelled.
[TableText] Yearly refresh
[TableText] Every sourced fact has a review cadence. During onboarding the customer selects the publication month/date for annual reports; automated reminders request the latest document and backend extraction prepares a review queue.
[TableText] WEF-style transformation map
[TableText] Include an interactive Regulatory Universe Map as a secondary discovery/impact view: program -> requirement -> obligation -> action/evidence. Nodes expand to cited details; filters and export are supported. It does not replace the answer-first dashboard or expose raw graph complexity by default.

[Heading1] 8. Five enduring principles
1. Two graphs, one join: shared knowledge, private state, obligation as the connection.
2. No unsourced fact and no unexplained answer.
3. Deterministic core, probabilistic edges, mandatory human ratification of the knowledge record.
4. Versions, not overwritten states: preserve the defence file.
5. Every surface answers one of the six questions and ends in an owned next step.
[Heading1] 9. What must be pressure-tested next
• Condition-language coverage: express the first 50 CBAM/EUDR/buyer-code requirements without special-case code, and regression-test them against golden profiles.
• Commercial interpretation of uncertainty: test whether customers read Requires judgment as credible candour or incomplete service, and define analyst response times accordingly.
• Quiet-product economics: validate that explicit all-clears, caught changes and a visible change ledger create enough felt value to support renewal without engagement theatre.
• Build order for two people: source snapshots/watchers -> workbench and schema -> evaluator -> checker/profile -> universe/register -> briefing -> calendar export.
