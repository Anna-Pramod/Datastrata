[Eyebrow] UI/UX MOCKUP BRIEF
[Title] DataStrata Regulatory Intelligence Platform
[Subtitle] A complete design brief for a high-fidelity, clickable product mockup covering the wedge, compliance-operations and assurance phases.


[Callout] Design outcome: make a complex, high-stakes regulatory system feel calm, bounded and continuously maintained. The user should understand where they stand, what changed and what to do next within four seconds.

[Heading1] 1. Product context and mockup objective
Product promise. DataStrata watches sustainability market-access rules for a specific organisation, determines what applies, explains why, converts obligations into owned work, maintains the evidence trail and makes assurance replayable.
Mockup objective. Create a coherent desktop-first experience demonstrating the complete product spine without implying that every subsystem launches at once. Stage 1 screens must feel fully usable; Stage 2 and Stage 3 screens must use the same objects, status logic and navigation so the product visibly expands rather than changes identity.

[Heading1] 3. Experience principles
1. Never start empty. Show a checker-fed preliminary universe or begin the guided profile flow immediately; do not display a blank analytics dashboard.
2. Every answer has a visible “Why?” path to facts, conditions, sources, versions and sign-offs.
3. State certainty explicitly. Separate Deterministic, Expert-signed and Requires judgment; never disguise missing facts as low confidence.
4. Give negative determinations equal weight. “Does not apply” is a valuable, exportable result, not an omitted row.
5. Use urgency sparingly. Red is reserved for blocked or materially late states; ordinary work is presented through runway and ownership.
6. Bound the user’s attention. The inbox ends with “You are current”; information and demands are never mixed.
7. No insight without a handle. Every gap/change links to an owner, deadline, decision or next step.
8. Optimise for reliance, not engagement. Weekly all-clear and change absorption matter more than daily active use.

[Heading2] Phase 1 - Declare: profile creation
Input strategy. The profile flow must support document-assisted, link-assisted and manual entry in one path. OCR/AI proposes facts but cannot commit them; users confirm or amend every extracted fact before it becomes sourced profile data.
[TableHeader] Screen
[TableHeader] Required components
[TableHeader] Key behaviour
[TableText] Choose profile method
[TableText] Cards: Upload public documents; Add public document/website link; Enter manually. Suggested document checklist by company type.
[TableText] Multiple methods may be combined. Explain privacy and that uploaded files are used for fact extraction, not assumed true.
[TableText] Document intake
[TableText] Drag/drop; URL field; document type; financial year; public/private toggle; processing state; replacement option.
[TableText] Suggested documents: annual report, audited accounts, sustainability report, product catalogue, certifications and export/product list.
[TableText] Extraction review
[TableText] Split view: document/page preview and extracted facts; source citation; confidence; exact/range selector; confirm/edit/reject; bulk confirm only for non-critical facts.
[TableText] Threshold-critical facts cannot be bulk-confirmed. A fact is not active until confirmed.
[TableText] Guided interview
[TableText] One question at a time; reason for asking; “use a range” option; “I do not know”; source attachment; progress based on determinations resolved, not question count.
[TableText] Questions are generated from unresolved applicability conditions. Do not ask irrelevant feature-driven questions.
[TableText] Profile review
[TableText] Entity tree; products/HS codes; facilities; trade lanes; buyers; commitments; sourced facts; missing/expiring items.
[TableText] Business Units cannot be used as legal scope anchors. Legal Entity, Product and Trade Lane are prominent.
[TableText] Refresh schedule
[TableText] For each recurring source: publication month/date, reminder lead time, owner and preferred channel.
[TableText] Automatically create annual refresh reminders; new document triggers backend extraction and a review queue, not silent overwrite.

[Callout] Range rule: ranges are acceptable for preliminary screening when the law uses broad thresholds. Exact sourced values are required when the customer sits near a threshold or when a definitive determination, filing or assurance statement depends on the number.

[TableText] Result badge; plain-language explanation; condition tree; profile facts; legal provisions; versions; evaluation time; sign-off; history.

[TableText] Regulatory Universe Map
[TableText] Interactive program-centred map; nodes for program, requirement, obligation, action/evidence; status/uncertainty coding; zoom, filter, search, expand/collapse, mini-map and list sync.
[TableText] Secondary view, not home. Clicking a node opens cited detail. Export current view to PDF/PNG and data to CSV; Stage 2 adds saved layouts and deeper customisation.

[Heading3] Regulatory Universe Map interaction specification
• Default graph is curated and shallow: Program -> customer scope -> applicable requirement/obligation. Do not expose every source/provision/cross-reference until the user expands a node.
• Map and list are two views of the same filtered state. Selecting or filtering in one updates the other.
• Node states: Applies, Does not apply, Missing fact, Requires judgment; obligation posture is shown only after instantiation.
• Click opens a side panel with summary, “why”, source, effective dates, affected entity/product/lane and related actions/evidence.
• Custom export: title, date, selected programs, selected node depth, legend, confidential watermark and orientation. Export must preserve citations or append a source register.
• Never use the map as a decorative “complexity theatre” visual. Its jobs are orientation, impact tracing, explanation and export.

[Heading1] 13. Component inventory
• Global shell: left navigation, entity/program scope switcher, search, notifications, help, user menu and persistent business-change action.
• State components: posture chip, applicability badge, warranty badge, impact badge, evidence state, runway indicator and blocker list.
• Trust components: source citation, version/effective-date block, “Why?” drawer, derivation tree, sign-off record and history timeline.
• Work components: owner picker, task card, plan timeline, calendar item, approval request, decision record and risk-acceptance form.
• Data components: filterable register, saved view, side drawer, comparison view, diff panel, source-document viewer and graph-map node.
• Communication components: weekly briefing card, explicit all-clear, analyst note, email preview and report/export configuration.
• Assurance components: scope builder, readiness blocker list, access boundary, reviewer portal, finding card and source register.
[Heading1] 14. Responsive, accessibility and trust requirements
• Desktop-first at 1440 px for the full operational product; responsive tablet at 1024 px; mobile focuses on briefing, all-clear, Needs you, task completion and evidence capture rather than graph/table administration.
• Meet WCAG 2.2 AA: keyboard navigation, visible focus, semantic headings, table alternatives, 4.5:1 text contrast, non-colour state indicators, accessible graph fallback and readable export output.
• All dates show timezone and unambiguous format; regulatory effective dates and customer due dates must not be visually conflated.
• Every destructive or scope-changing action has preview and confirmation. Historical records are superseded, not deleted.
• Customer-private and shared-knowledge data are visually distinguishable where relevant. Any external sharing screen shows exactly what the recipient will see and when access expires.
• Loading and error states must preserve truth: if AI composition is unavailable, show structured record data; if source freshness is delayed, show the last warranted state and the latency issue explicitly.

[Heading1] 17. Acceptance criteria for the mockup
• A new user can reach a preliminary regulatory universe without entering exact values for every fact, while understanding which results remain preliminary.
• The document-assisted profile flow visibly requires user confirmation before extracted facts become authoritative.
• The map, list and determination detail show the same result and can explain every node/row with sources and versions.
• The Home screen answers “where do we stand?” and “what changed?” within four seconds without a single aggregate compliance score.
• A regulatory change can be traced from source event to changed determination, obligation, task and briefing.
• A business change can be evaluated as a scenario and then confirmed without overwriting history.
• A completed task without accepted evidence does not falsely improve compliance posture.
• An assurance package can be scoped and shared without exposing unrelated customer data.
• Stage 1 feels complete, while Stage 2 and Stage 3 additions visibly reuse the same objects and workflows.
• The internal workbench demonstrates that AI drafts and humans ratify; no AI action publishes directly into the warranted record.
[Heading1] 18. Do not imply in the mockup
• DataStrata calculates emissions, completes questionnaires as a service, or replaces legal counsel. It identifies duties, methods, proof and decisions; adjacent calculation/advisory work is partnered.
• The product is a document vault. Evidence is represented as a registry with provenance and pointers; managed custody is optional and late.
• A generic chatbot is the source of truth. Ask DataStrata is a cited lens over warranted records, not an open-ended regulatory oracle.
• The transformation map is the main dashboard or a decorative network visual. It is a secondary orientation and impact-analysis tool.
• Stage 1 includes public API/MCP, enterprise integration theatre, supplier networks or fully native GRC workflow. These remain visibly stage-gated.
[Heading1] 19. Questions for prototype testing
